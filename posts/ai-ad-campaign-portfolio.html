I Was an AI Skeptic. Then I Used It to Plan an Ad Campaign for My Portfolio.

Like a lot of marketing students, I worried about what generative AI meant for our future. Was it going to make our skills obsolete before we even graduated? But throughout my digital marketing course, I reached a turning point: AI isn’t here to replace us—it’s here to amplify us.

Figure 1: Designing an AI prompt workflow that balances machine leverage with human creative vision and emotional resonance.

At the end of the day, AI isn't going to do our thinking for us. You still need a human in the driver's seat to steer the strategy, bring creative taste, and make sure the words actually sound like someone talking to you. If you ignore AI completely, you miss out on huge speed and efficiency gains. But if you rely on it blindly, you end up with robotic, soulless content that makes people scroll right past.

That’s why I decided to make peace with the tech and put it to the test. Today, I turned a class exercise into a real-world experiment: planning an AI-powered ad campaign for my own project, the Digital Marketing Lab.

Here is the exact 4-step framework I followed to align my AI prompts with the customer journey—and what I learned about making AI ads that feel genuinely human.

01

Funnel Matching with a "Respect-First" Mindset

When I first sat down to plan an ad for my portfolio site, my initial thought was to go big: "Check out my portfolio!" or "Hire me for your next campaign!"

But then I took a step back and did a basic social media reality check: Why do people open social media?

Nobody logs on to look at ads. Nobody opens Instagram or TikTok thinking, "I can't wait to see what strangers try to sell me today." When an ad interrupts someone's feed demanding attention, their instant reaction is annoyance: "How fast can I skip this?"

✕ The Traditional Pushy Pitch (High Friction)

"Look at my portfolio! Hire me for your next campaign!"

Demanding immediate commitment from cold traffic triggers instant fatigue. When you ask strangers for their time or money before giving them a reason to care, their first instinct is to skip. It treats user attention as an obligation rather than an earned privilege.

✓ The Respect-First Value Exchange (Empathy)

"Here's a free 5-minute prompt guide to fix robotic AI copy."

Offering a quick, practical solution up front turns an interruption into an invitation. You give genuine value first, establish trust, and open the door to a real relationship before asking for anything in return.

If I want someone's time, my ad needs to be humble, educational, and respectful. Rather than forcing a hard pitch, it should feel like an invitation: offering genuine value before asking for anything in return.

Matching Goal to Funnel Stage

  • Campaign Goal: Drive traffic to my portfolio site and collect email sign-ups from fellow students and growth creators.
  • The Lead Magnet Offer: A free downloadable guide titled "The 5-Minute AI Ad Prompt Playbook."
  • Funnel Stage Match: Consideration Stage (Middle of Funnel).

Asking a cold audience to hire you at Top-of-Funnel is like asking someone to marry you on the first date. By matching a free, high-value resource to the Consideration stage, I create a smooth, low-friction value exchange.

💡 The Marketer's Takeaway

Generative AI can churn out dozens of ad concepts in seconds, but it has no instinct for empathy or social media fatigue. When content feels purely synthetic, viewers put their guard up immediately and disengage. As marketers, our real value isn't pressing the generate button—it's directing the story with empathy and ensuring every touchpoint delivers genuine value.

02

Crafting a Message That Sounds Like a Conversation, Not "Plain Text"

Once the offer was locked in, I needed to draft the ad copy.

If you ask an AI for "high-converting ad copy," it usually spits out generic lines like "Revolutionize your marketing with AI!" or "Struggling to write ad copy?" But nobody reads lines like that and thinks, "Wow, a real person wrote this." It sounds like cold, plain text with nobody on the other side of the screen.

"Have you ever scrolled past an AI ad and felt like you were just reading plain text? There’s no warmth, no personality—it feels like there isn’t even a real person on the other side of the screen. When I first tried using AI for ads, that’s exactly what happened to me. But then I figured out a framework that makes the ad feel like an actual conversation with a real person..."

The 3-Part Prompting Framework

To train ChatGPT to sound like an approachable peer rather than a stiff sales robot, I built a structured three-part prompting framework:

1. Define a Relatable Persona

Instead of telling the AI to "write an ad," I gave it a grounded role: "Act as a friendly, curious digital marketing student having a 1-on-1 chat with a fellow creator." That simple instruction strips away the corporate jargon and anchors the copy in an authentic, conversational voice.

2. Follow the Problem-Agitate-Solution Arc

To keep the message punchy and focused within 15 seconds, I structured the copy around a classic storytelling framework:

  • Problem: Call out the cold, robotic feeling of plain-text AI ads that flood our feeds.
  • Agitate: Remind the viewer that soulless ads cause instant skips and waste valuable budget.
  • Solution: Introduce my free prompt playbook as the practical fix to add warmth and personality.

3. Enforce Strict Negative Constraints

One of the most effective prompting habits is telling AI what not to write. I explicitly banned marketing cliches like "unleash," "supercharge," and "game-changer." If a phrase wouldn't sound natural coming out of a friend's mouth at a coffee shop, it got cut.

📋 Reproducible AI Ad Prompt 
Act as a humble, friendly digital marketing student sharing a personal breakthrough.
Write a 15-second conversational script using the Problem-Agitate-Solution (PAS) framework.
- Hook: Ask if they ever scroll past AI ads that feel like soulless plain text.
- Agitate: Acknowledge that robotic copy repels social users and burns ad budget.
- Solution: Point to a 5-minute prompt playbook linked in the bio.
Constraint: Zero buzzwords (no 'unleash', 'supercharge', 'game-changer'). Pacing must sound conversational and human.

The Human Polish

Even with strict prompt rules, the initial draft was only about 80% there. That's why having a human in the loop is essential. I read the draft out loud, smoothed out awkward phrasing, adjusted the pauses, and trimmed out filler words until it flowed like an everyday conversation.

The 3-Step Production Stack

To produce this format efficiently without needing a studio crew, I mapped out a straightforward three-step workflow:

Step 1: Scriptwriting & Hook Testing (ChatGPT)

I started with ChatGPT to draft the conversational script, test different opening hooks, and time each sentence so the whole message lands smoothly within 15 seconds.

Step 2: Creator Persona & Visual Framing (Gemini)

Next, I used Gemini to create a realistic, approachable student creator persona. Instead of an over-polished corporate spokesperson or generic stock photo, the goal was someone relatable—a creator sitting at her desk with natural ambient lighting and friendly, direct eye contact.

Step 3: Video Synthesis & Quality Control

After pairing the voiceover with video rendering, the final step was human review. Checking the lip synchronization, facial expressions, and natural breathing rhythm ensures the output feels authentic rather than falling into the awkward uncanny valley.

Quality Control: Defeating the "Uncanny Valley"

While AI tools can generate video assets in minutes, you can't just press render and walk away. If an avatar's lip-sync drifts or the facial expressions feel frozen, viewers immediately notice the "uncanny valley." It creates an uneasy impression that damages brand trust. Taking the time to review every cut, check the lip sync, and ensure the energy feels authentic is what separates a gimmick from an effective ad.

04

Audience Targeting & The "Data Diagnostic" Mindset

A great ad is useless if it lands in front of the wrong people. For Step 4, I set up a targeted distribution and optimization plan.

Audience Targeting (Middle of Funnel)

Instead of broadcasting to everyone, targeting focuses on high-intent interest groups across Meta and LinkedIn:

  • Primary Interests: "Digital Marketing," "Content Strategy," "Generative AI," and "Social Media Advertising."
  • Lookalike & Retargeting: Retargeting visitors who previously viewed my portfolio and creating a Lookalike Audience from users who download the prompt guide.

What Happens If I Get Zero Sign-Ups?

This was one of my biggest questions during the class exercise: If I run this ad and get zero conversions, did I fail?

In performance marketing, a campaign that yields zero sign-ups isn't a failure—it’s diagnostic data. Marketing is continuous hypothesis testing. When a campaign runs, metrics isolate the exact point of friction:

Scenario A: High Clicks, 0 Sign-ups

Creative Works • Landing Page Friction

The AI video hook succeeded at capturing attention and generating interest, but the portfolio landing page or guide offer did not deliver on expectations. Optimize the page copy or simplify the signup form.

Scenario B: Low Clicks Overall

Hook Missed • Audience Misalignment

The opening 3-second video hook or audience targeting missed the mark. Because AI allows rapid iteration, generate 3 new hook variations and run an A/B test.

Because AI allows us to generate multiple creative variations rapidly, we can run A/B tests across different opening hooks to find what truly resonates.

05

3 Pro-Marketer Takeaways on AI Advertising

To wrap up the exercise, here are three essential lessons every creator, business associate, and marketer should keep in mind when working with AI:

✦ Framework 1

The Hybrid Model Wins Every Time

In my tests and real-world campaign observations, hybrid workflows—where human strategy directs AI variation and testing—beat purely machine-generated ads every single time. Strategy belongs to the marketer; speed and leverage belong to the machine.

✦ Framework 2

Avoid "AI Washing" & Hype

Watch out for "AI washing"—making exaggerated claims about what AI or your product can actually do. Consumers and regulators are quick to spot overblown promises. Honest, transparent copywriting always builds more trust and long-term brand equity.

✦ Framework 3

Originality & Creative Direction

Purely machine-generated outputs lack a unique point of view and quickly feel generic. To build real brand loyalty and own your creative assets, meaningful human storytelling, thoughtful editing, and original curation are non-negotiable.
